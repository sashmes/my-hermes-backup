# Dossier: California Handyman & Long Beach City Local Launch Compliance
**Last Updated:** June 24, 2026

This reference dossier outlines the strict legal, financial, and municipal guidelines for launching and operating a handyman business (licensed or unlicensed) in **Long Beach, California (Los Angeles County)**.

---

## 1. California State Regulations (CSLB)

In California, construction and contracting are strictly regulated by the **Contractors State License Board (CSLB)**. There is no specific "handyman license." An operator is either an unlicensed service provider or a licensed specialty/general contractor.

### The $1,000 Unlicensed Limit (AB 2622)
*   **The Law:** Effective **January 1, 2025**, Assembly Bill 2622 officially raised the unlicensed contracting threshold from $500 to **$1,000**. This was the first increase in the limit since 2005.
*   **Labor & Materials Included:** The $1,000 limit includes **both labor and materials** combined. Even if the customer purchases all the materials, their fair market value is counted towards your $1,000 project limit.
*   **The Solo Operator Mandate:** Under AB 2622, unlicensed operators **must work completely solo**. If you hire helpers, employees, or subcontractors, you are no longer exempt and must hold a valid state contractor's license.
*   **No Job Splitting:** Breaking up a larger job into smaller invoices to skirt the $1,000 limit is a misdemeanor (e.g., splitting a $1,800 project into two $900 agreements).
*   **No Sub-Tasks of Larger Projects:** You cannot contract to perform a small part (e.g., a $400 task) of a larger project whose total value exceeds $1,000 (e.g., a kitchen remodel).
*   **No Permitted Work:** Unlicensed handypeople cannot do any work that requires a municipal permit (building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical), regardless of the cost of the project.

### Advertising Disclosures for Unlicensed Operators
Unlicensed handypeople are legally allowed to advertise, but **must** state they are unlicensed. Business & Professions Code §7027.2 requires including this exact statement in all advertisements (Craigslist, Yelp, Google, flyers, business cards, vehicle wraps):

> *"State law requires that anyone who contracts to do construction work be licensed by the Contractors State License Board in the License Class of the person's profession. Licensure is not required if the project price is under $1,000."*

### Criminal Penalties for Violations
The CSLB has a dedicated undercover team, the **Statewide Investigative Fraud Team (SWIFT)**, which regularly conducts stings in Southern California.
*   **First Offense:** Misdemeanor, fines up to $5,000, and up to 6 months in county jail.
*   **Second Offense:** Mandatory minimum of 90 days in county jail.
*   **Collections Penalty:** Under state law, unlicensed contractors **cannot sue or file a mechanic's lien** to collect unpaid balances. Contracts entered into by unlicensed contractors are legally unenforceable.

## 2. Municipal Compliance: City of Long Beach

### Business License and Business Tax Account
*   **Required:** Anyone conducting business inside Long Beach city limits must obtain a city business license. This applies to home-based businesses, commercial suites, and out-of-city contractors performing work within the city.
*   **Filing Bureau:** City of Long Beach Department of Financial Management, Business License Division (City Hall, 411 W. Ocean Blvd, 4th Floor, Long Beach, CA 90802. Phone: 562-570-6211. Email: `lbbiz@longbeach.gov`).
*   **Filing Method:** Online via **Long Beach BizPort**.
*   **Fees:** First-year taxes and processing fees are charged upfront. Base tax starts around $45 to $100 depending on exact classification, plus a $4 state-mandated ADA fee.

### Home Occupation Permit (Zoning)
If operating out of a residential property in Long Beach:
*   The business must be incidental to the residential use.
*   Commercial vehicles over 10,000 lbs cannot be parked in residential zones.
*   No bulk outdoor storage of materials, tools, or hazardous substances.
*   No employee gatherings or customer traffic to the home.

### Stormwater Compliance (Senate Bill 205)
*   You must fill out the supplemental National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) / Stormwater form during application. For a handyman, you must verify your operations do not dump wash water or toxic chemicals into municipal storm drains.

## 3. Entity Structural Comparison (CA Specifics)

| Criteria | Sole Proprietorship | Limited Liability Company (LLC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Setup Cost** | Extremely low (No SOS filing fees) | Moderate ($70 SOS filing fee + publication fees) |
| **Annual State Fee** | None | **$800 annual minimum Franchise Tax** (FTB) |
| **Personal Liability** | Unlimited personal liability | Protected (Liability limited to business assets) |
| **Bonds (If Licensed)** | $25,000 Contractor Bond | $25,000 Contractor Bond + **$100,000 LLC Bond** |
| **Tax Filing** | Schedule C on Personal 1040 | Schedule C (Single-member) or Form 1065 (Multi-member) |

### Fictitious Business Name (FBN / DBA)
If doing business under any name other than your legal surname (e.g., *"Long Beach Handyman Services"*), you must:
1.  Register the FBN with the **Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk** ($26+ base fee).
2.  Publish the registered FBN statement in an approved local LA County newspaper once a week for four consecutive weeks.

## 4. Required Insurance Coverage

*   **General Liability Insurance:** Standard coverage recommendation is $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate. This protects you if you accidentally cause property damage (e.g., drill into a water pipe) or cause bodily injury. Costs roughly $400–$900 annually for a handyman.
*   **Commercial Auto Insurance:** Highly recommended. Standard personal auto insurance policies exclude commercial hauling of tools, ladders, and materials.
*   **Workers' Compensation:** Unlicensed handymen cannot legally hire employees. If you later obtain a state contractor's license, California is phasing in a universal mandate requiring workers' comp for *all* contractors, even those with no employees, to prevent unlicensed under-the-table hires.

## 5. The Transition Path to a CSLB License

If you grow beyond the $1,000 per-job limit, you must get a contractor's license. The most suitable class for a general handyman is the **Class B-2 Residential Remodeling Contractor License**.

### B-2 Scope of Work
*   Permits remodeling and rehabilitation on existing wood-frame residential structures.
*   Requires the project to involve three or more unrelated trades (e.g., carpentry, painting, and drywall).
*   Prohibits structural alterations to load-bearing walls or building brand-new homes (which requires a Class B General Building license).

### CSLB Requirements
1.  **Experience:** Show four years of journey-level experience within the last ten years (verifiable by a licensed contractor, employer, or client).
2.  **Fees:** $450 application fee + $200 license activation fee.
3.  **Exams:** Pass the Law & Business exam + the B-2 trade exam.
4.  **Bonds:** File a $25,000 contractor's surety bond with the CSLB.
